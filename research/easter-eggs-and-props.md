# Flanaverse easter eggs, props, and cross-work connections

*Research compiled 2026-11-02. Covers Absentia (2011) through Carrie (Prime Video, released 2026-10-07).*

## How to read this file

Every entry carries one status label:

- **CONFIRMED**: Mike Flanagan, a credited crew member, or an official production source has said it on the record (interview, his own social post, his Tumblr Q&A, a screen credit).
- **WIDELY REPORTED**: two or more independent outlets describe it, but no one from the production has confirmed it.
- **FAN THEORY**: one outlet, a fan post, or an interpretation. Treat as unverified until someone checks the frame.

Timestamps come from the cited articles and vary between streaming encodes. Check them against the actual episode before publishing them on the site.

Episode numbering follows Wikipedia's episode tables.

### Things in the brief that did not check out

## 1. Recurring props and objects

### 1.1 The Lasser Glass (the *Oculus* mirror)

The haunted mirror from *Oculus*, designed by Bruce Larsen, with a frame Flanagan describes as "writhing humanoid forms." Flanagan has said he keeps hiding it to keep the mythology alive for a possible *Oculus* follow-up, which is stuck because rights are murky after Relativity Media's bankruptcy. He pitched the original as a "portable Overlook Hotel" ([Collider](https://collider.com/mike-flanagan-easter-eggs/); [SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/1402882/fall-house-usher-oculus-mirror-lasser-glass-cameo/); [Static on the TV](https://staticonthetv.substack.com/p/oculus-and-the-lasser-glass-mike)).

Sources disagree on how many physical mirrors exist. One report says two were built for *Oculus* (past and present timelines), one now at Flanagan's home and one that travels to sets; another says one broke ([search summary of Collider/Filmifeed](https://www.filmifeed.com/the-oculus-mirror-has-been-hiding-in-mike-flanagans-other-movies-and-shows/), low-reliability source). Treat the count as unsettled.

### 1.2 Maddie Young's novel *Midnight Mass*

- **Appears in:** *Hush* (2016), *Gerald's Game* (2017); pays off as *Midnight Mass* (2021).
- **What:** A hardback horror novel credited to Maddie Young (Kate Siegel), the deaf author in *Hush*. The back cover copy mentions "the 512 souls on sleepy Crocket[t]" island and a church that puts "Riley face to face with a terrifying secret." The cover also carries a fake blurb attributed to producer Trevor Macy.
- **Context:** In *Hush*, a friend returns Maddie's copy and we see the back cover. In *Gerald's Game*, the same cover sits on the shelf above the bed, and Jessie grabs it to throw at the stray dog.
- **Status:** WIDELY REPORTED (the props are on screen; Flanagan developed *Midnight Mass* as a novel, then a script, for more than a decade).
- **Sources:** [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/midnight-mass-novel-easter-egg-connects-hush-gerlads-game/); [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/midnight-mass-book-easter-eggs-hush-geralds-game-explained/); [Capital/PopBuzz](https://www.capitalfm.com/news/tv-film/midnight-mass-hush-connected-geralds-game/); [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/midnight-mass-mike-flanagan-stephen-king-doctor-sleep-hush-1480659); [ScreenCrush](https://screencrush.com/geralds-game-easter-eggs/)
- **Note for the site:** Because *Midnight Mass* is fiction inside *Hush* and *Gerald's Game*, the series itself can't share their universe. CBR and Capital both point this out.

### 1.3 Maddie's alternate endings (Hush) foreshadow Erin's death (Midnight Mass)

- **Appears in:** *Hush*; *Midnight Mass* Ep. 7 "Book VII: Revelation."
- **What:** In *Hush*, Maddie works through endings for her next book. Her inner voice says: "Erin dies. Can't kill Erin. That would piss 'em off. Didn't set it up properly." A briefly shown manuscript page appears to show Erin being drained by a vampire.
- **Context:** Erin Greene (Kate Siegel) does die in the *Midnight Mass* finale.
- **Status:** FAN THEORY as foreshadowing (Screen Rant's inference); the *Hush* dialogue itself is on screen.
- **Source:** [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/midnight-mass-erin-death-hush-movie-setup/)

### 1.4 The *Hush* mask

- **Appears in:** *Hush* (2016); *The Fall of the House of Usher* Ep. 2 "The Masque of the Red Death."
- **What:** The killer's smiling white mask. Flanagan says Bruce Larsen made it from the mold of the Canker Man's face in *Before I Wake*, softened to look more human (see 1.5).
- **Context:** At Perry's masquerade orgy, Jenny wears the *Hush* killer's mask. Everyone but one guest dies by the end of the episode.
- **Status:** WIDELY REPORTED (Screen Rant, Creepy Catalog). Not confirmed by Flanagan.
- **Sources:** [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/fall-house-usher-easter-eggs-references-hidden-details/); [Creepy Catalog](https://creepycatalog.com/hidden-details-easter-eggs-the-fall-of-the-house-of-usher-on-netflix/)

### 1.5 Canker Man → Hush mask (one mold, two films)

- **Appears in:** *Before I Wake* (2016), *Hush* (2016).
- **What:** Flanagan on his Tumblr: "The HUSH mask was designed by Bruce Larsen, who also designed the OCULUS mirror, and the Canker Man character from BEFORE I WAKE. The mask in HUSH is actually based on the Canker Man's face from that movie. We used the mold for the creature design that Bruce had made." He adds the mask was *not* modelled on Kate Siegel's face.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED.
- **Source:** [Flanagan's Tumblr Q&A](https://www.tumblr.com/flanaganfilm/tagged/canker%20man)

### 1.7 The red door

- **Appears in:** *Hill House* (the Red Room, whole series; reveal in Ep. 10 "Silence Lay Steadily"); *Carrie* (2026).
- **What:** The locked Red Room door at Hill House, which takes a different form for each Crain. In *Carrie*, the White family's front door is red in the police-discovery scene, and Principal Grayle's (Matthew Lillard) prom shirt shows a red door. Flanagan's post-2024 production company is called Red Room Pictures.
- **Status:** Red Room in Hill House is canon. Carrie red-door nods: WIDELY REPORTED (Pop Culture Planet, Geek Vibes Nation); not Flanagan-confirmed.
- **Sources:** [Pop Culture Planet](https://www.popcultureplanet.com/articles/zw51mwdjt5eg0zhk95asd7w3axerrj); [Geek Vibes Nation](https://geekvibesnation.com/carrie-season-1-easter-eggs/); [Flanagan bio (Red Room Pictures)](https://mikeflanaganfilm.com/bio)

- **Status:** WIDELY REPORTED (TV Guide summary of Flanagan's answers, GamesRadar, Looper). No source shows the hat recurring in another Flanagan work.

## 2. Hidden ghosts and visual eggs inside individual works

### 2.1 Hill House: hidden background ghosts (overview)

- **What:** Flanagan says hiding ghosts in plain sight in every episode was part of the original pitch, with "dozens" per episode. Ghost extras were on set daily. He also said "we tried to use design elements that look like human faces everywhere we could" (wallpaper, light fixtures, doorknobs). Writer Jeff Howard says the team knows why each ghost is there, even if the show never explains it.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED.
- **Sources:** [Looper](https://www.looper.com/138439/the-haunting-of-hill-house-hidden-details-you-missed/); [Higgypop](https://www.higgypop.com/news/hidden-ghosts-haunting-of-hill-house/); [IMDb: 43 hidden ghosts](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt9287308/)
- **Counts disagree:** NME lists 33; IMDb Originals lists 43; Looper says "over three dozen."

### 3.14 Recurring monologue structure

## 7. Recurring themes

Short notes for the site's theme pages. Strongest carriers in bold.

1. **Grief and the people left behind.** The engine of nearly everything: a husband declared dead in absentia (**Absentia**), siblings each stuck at a different stage of grief (**Hill House**; Flanagan told EW grief's universality shaped the writers' room), a dead son (Before I Wake), and Bly's love story told by a widow. [Looper](https://www.looper.com/138439/the-haunting-of-hill-house-hidden-details-you-missed/)
2. **Addiction and recovery.** Flanagan got sober while making **Doctor Sleep** and calls Riley in **Midnight Mass** "my most thinly veiled avatar for myself." Also Luke's heroin addiction (**Hill House**), Joe Collie (Midnight Mass), and the Usher opioid empire built on Ligadone, modelled on the Sacklers (**Usher**). [Collider](https://collider.com/doctor-sleep-alcoholism-mike-flanagan-comments/); [Dread Central](https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/465626/a-sleepless-weekend-mike-flanagan-on-doctor-sleep-and-his-path-to-sobriety/); [Flanagan's blog](https://flanaganfilm.blog/post/722300928619921408/you-have-spoken-about-dealing-with-addiction-in)
3. **Faith, zealotry, and the church.** Flanagan was an altar boy on Governors Island, NY. **Midnight Mass** is his "most personal" project; **Carrie**'s Margaret and Midnight Mass's Bev Keane share an actor; Ouija's Father Tom and Usher's pious Eliza fill in the edges. [SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/613478/mike-flanagan-calls-midnight-mass-his-most-personal-project-yet/); [Collider interview](https://collider.com/midnight-mass-mike-flanagan-trevor-macy-interview/)
4. **The monologue as confession.** See 3.14. Strongest in **Midnight Mass**, then Hill House and Usher.
5. **Families that break their children.** Oculus, **Hill House**, Gerald's Game (an abusive father), **Usher** (a dynasty that destroys its heirs), **Carrie** (mother and daughter). TV Tropes lists the dysfunctional family as a Flanagan constant. [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Creator/MikeFlanagan)
6. **The dead as company, not threat.** The dead crowd around the living in Oculus and Hill House Ep. 6, but by Hill House's finale they are a comfort ("the rest is confetti"). Midnight Club makes the dying kids keep each other company, and **Life of Chuck** ends on a whole life worth having lived.
7. **Ghosts as memory.** Steven's "a ghost is a wish" (**Hill House**); in **Bly Manor** the dead lose their faces as they forget who they were, and Dani becomes a ghost in Jamie's memory. Life of Chuck's "I contain multitudes" carries the same idea without a ghost in sight.
8. **Mortality faced head-on.** **Midnight Club** (a hospice for teenagers), **Midnight Mass** (Erin's and Riley's death speeches), **Life of Chuck** (a universe ending with one man). Flanagan has said in interviews that confronting his own fear of death drove Midnight Mass. [CinemaBlend](https://www.cinemablend.com/television/2574223/midnight-mass-mike-flanagan-used-netflix-confront-darkest-fear)
9. **Women trapped by men.** Jessie in **Gerald's Game**, Morrie in **Usher**, Rebecca under Peter Quint in **Bly**, Carrie under Margaret and Chris's crowd. Usher's Ep. 6 *Gerald's Game* egg (1.13) makes the link explicit.
10. **Time out of order.** Braided timelines in Oculus and **Hill House**, Bly's memory loops, and Life of Chuck's reverse-order acts. [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/mike-flanagan-haunting-of-hill-house-horror-tv-time-travel/)
