# Mike Flanagan filmography (verified research)

*Compiled 2026-11-02. Facts checked against Wikipedia (live article text and raw wikitext), TMDB pages, trade press (Deadline, THR, TheWrap, Variety) and Flanagan's official site (mikeflanaganfilm.com). Where sources disagree, both values are given.*

## How to read this file

- **TMDB** values come from fetching `themoviedb.org/search?query=...` and then the candidate page itself. Each ID was confirmed by checking the page title/year, and for most entries that "Mike Flanagan" appears in the credits. Format is `movie/<id>` or `tv/<id>`, which maps to `https://www.themoviedb.org/<type>/<id>`.
- **Roles** use these codes: D = director, W = writer, E = editor, P = producer, EP = executive producer, C = creator/developer, SR = showrunner.
- Spoilers throughout.

## Part 1: Core canon (directed or created)

### 1. Makebelieve (2000)

- **Type:** Feature-length student film, made at Towson University. Digital feature.
- **Roles:** Director, writer, editor, composer (not producer) per the Wikipedia filmography table.
- **Source material:** Original.
- **Premise:** Coming-of-age drama about the loves, lives and infidelities of four college friends while they stage a production of *Romeo and Juliet* (TMDB synopsis). TMDB notes it "is not currently known to be publicly available for viewing."
- **Distribution:** None known. Billed as an independent film on its 2000 release.
- **TMDB:** movie/484346
- **Wikipedia:** No standalone article; listed in the [Mike Flanagan filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Flanagan_(filmmaker)#Filmography).
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia, Flanagan filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Flanagan_(filmmaker)); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/movie/484346); [Towson University archives](https://archives.towson.edu/wiki/university-leaders/mike-flanagan).

### 2. Still Life (2001)

- **Type:** Feature-length student film, Towson / Baltimore area.
- **Roles:** Director, writer, editor, producer, composer.
- **Source material:** Original.
- **Premise:** Four student photographers work on one final project before graduation (secondary summaries only; no primary synopsis found).
- **TMDB:** No entry found. Searches for "Still Life" returned only unrelated titles.
- **Wikipedia:** Listed in the Flanagan filmography only.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia, Flanagan filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Flanagan_(filmmaker)); [Towson University archives](https://archives.towson.edu/wiki/university-leaders/mike-flanagan).

### 3. Ghosts of Hamilton Street (2003)

- **Type:** Feature-length student film; described as award-winning.
- **Roles:** Director, writer, editor.
- **Source material:** Original.
- **Premise:** A troubled writer questions his sanity as people in his life vanish without a trace, and he seems to be the only one who notices. Each disappearance drops him into a version of the world where that person never existed. Once he works out the order of the disappearances, he tries to save the people he loves (TMDB synopsis). Not publicly available.
- **TMDB:** movie/430253
- **Sources:** [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/movie/430253); [Wikipedia, Flanagan filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Flanagan_(filmmaker)); [Empire profile](https://empireonline.com/people/mike-flanagan).

### 4. Oculus: Chapter 3 – The Man with the Plan (2005 or 2006)

- **Type:** Short film.
- **Roles:** Director, writer, producer; editor uncredited.
- **Source material:** Original. Conceived as one chapter of a planned series of shorts about the mirror; when financing for the series fell through, Flanagan made this chapter alone to prove he could direct horror.
- **Budget / cast:** Made for $1,500; stars Scott Graham (who later appears in *Absentia*).
- **Premise:** A man locks himself in a room with an antique mirror, cameras and safeguards, to prove the mirror is haunted and kills people. It is the template for the feature's "experiment" storyline.
- **Release:** Festival circuit. Its festival popularity led to the feature *Oculus*.
- **Date conflict:** The Wikipedia filmography table and TMDB say **2006**; the body text of the Wikipedia *Oculus* article says **2005**.
- **TMDB:** movie/264833
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia, Oculus (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oculus_(film)); [Wikipedia, Flanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Flanagan_(filmmaker)); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/movie/264833).

### 5. Absentia (2011)

- **Type:** Feature film. 91 min.
- **Roles:** Director, writer, editor, producer (with Justin Gordon and Morgan Peter Brown). First feature as director.
- **Distributor:** Phase 4 Films (North American DVD/VOD). Production company Fallback Plan Productions.
- **Budget:** $70,000, with principal photography funded through Kickstarter.
- **Premiere:** March 3, 2011, Fargo Film Festival, followed by a long 2011 festival run (won Best Feature at Maelstrom and Best Horror Feature at Shriekfest).
- **Source material:** Original.
- **Premise:** Pregnant Tricia is about to have her husband Daniel, missing for seven years, declared dead in absentia. Her younger sister Callie, a recovering addict, moves in and has an encounter in the pedestrian tunnel near the house. Daniel then reappears, emaciated and traumatised. The tunnel turns out to be the lair of an unseen, insect-like entity that has been taking people for decades. It takes Daniel again, then Tricia, and in the end Callie herself.
- **Collaborators:** DP Rustin Cerveny; music Ryan David Leack; cast Courtney Bell, Katie Parker, Doug Jones, Scott Graham; Flanagan's brother James appears as Jamie Lambert.
- **TMDB:** movie/74777
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absentia_(film)); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/movie/74777).

### 6. Oculus (2013 festival / 2014 US release)

- **Type:** Feature film. 103 min.
- **Roles:** Director, editor, co-writer (screenplay with Jeff Howard). Not producer.
- **Producers:** Trevor Macy and Marc D. Evans. Companies: Intrepid Pictures, Blumhouse Productions, MICA Entertainment (WWE Studios also credited).
- **Distributor:** Relativity Media (US), Sierra/Affinity (international).
- **Dates:** TIFF premiere **September 5, 2013** per article text, **September 8, 2013** per the infobox (Wikipedia contradicts itself). US release April 11, 2014. TMDB lists the film as 2014.
- **Budget / box office:** $5M budget; about $44M worldwide.
- **Source material:** Flanagan's own short *Oculus: Chapter 3*.
- **Premise:** Tim Russell is released at 21 from the psychiatric hospital where he was held after shooting his father as a boy. His sister Kaylie has tracked down the Lasser Glass, an antique mirror she blames for their parents' deaths in 2002, and sets up a room with cameras, timers and a weighted "kill switch" anchor to prove its power and destroy it. The film cuts between the siblings' present-day night and the childhood events, with the mirror increasingly blurring which is which. The mirror tricks Tim into triggering the anchor, killing Kaylie; he is arrested, repeating the past.
- **Collaborators:** DP Michael Fimognari; music The Newton Brothers; cast Karen Gillan, Brenton Thwaites, Katee Sackhoff, Rory Cochrane, Annalise Basso, Kate Siegel.
- **TMDB:** movie/157547. Do not use movie/385938, which is a different "Oculus (2013)" about a haunted doll.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oculus_(film)); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/movie/157547).

### 7. Hush (2016)

- **Type:** Feature film. **81 min** per Wikipedia; one Daily Dead listing says 87 min.
- **Roles:** Director, editor, co-writer with Kate Siegel (who stars).
- **Producers:** Trevor Macy, Jason Blum. Companies: Blumhouse Productions, Intrepid Pictures.
- **Platform:** Netflix bought worldwide rights before the festival.
- **Dates:** World premiere SXSW March 12, 2016; Netflix release April 8, 2016.
- **Source material:** Original.
- **Premise:** Maddie Young, a deaf and mute horror novelist living alone in the woods, is targeted by a masked man with a crossbow after he kills her neighbour Sarah on her doorstep. Because she can't hear him, he toys with her rather than kill her straight away. The film is a home-invasion cat-and-mouse; Maddie survives by thinking like one of her own characters and kills him with a corkscrew. Easter egg: the novel on Maddie's screen is *Midnight Mass*, using text from Flanagan's real unpublished manuscript.
- **Collaborators:** DP James Kniest; music The Newton Brothers; cast Kate Siegel, John Gallagher Jr., Michael Trucco, Samantha Sloyan.
- **TMDB:** movie/376570
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hush_(2016_feature_film)); [Dread Central on SXSW premiere](https://dreadcentral.com/news/379734/horror-history-mike-flanagans-hush-premiere-at-sxsw-in-2016); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/movie/376570).

### 8. Before I Wake (2016 international / 2018 US)

- **Type:** Feature film. 97 min. Released as *Somnia* in some territories.
- **Roles:** Director, editor, co-writer with Jeff Howard.
- **Producers:** Trevor Macy, Sam Englebardt, William D. Johnson. Companies: Intrepid Pictures, Demarest Films, MICA Entertainment.
- **Release history (messy):** Relativity Media acquired it in 2014 and dated it for May 8, 2015, then September 25, 2015; the company's bankruptcy pulled it. Later Relativity dates (April 8, 2016; September 9, 2016) were also abandoned, and Relativity never released it. Some international theatrical releases from April 2016; Fantasia International Film Festival July 31, 2016; Netflix outside the US April 28, 2017; **Netflix US January 5, 2018**. TMDB lists it as 2016.
- **Source material:** Original.
- **Premise:** Jessie and Mark Hobson, grieving a son who drowned in the bath, foster eight-year-old Cody, who is terrified of sleep. His dreams become real while he sleeps: butterflies fill the house, and the dead son appears. Jessie starts to exploit this. Cody's nightmares produce the "Canker Man", which consumes people, including Mark. Jessie learns the Canker Man is Cody's warped memory of his mother dying of cancer, and she adopts him and helps him face it.
- **Collaborators:** DP Michael Fimognari; music Danny Elfman and The Newton Brothers; cast Kate Bosworth, Thomas Jane, Jacob Tremblay, Annabeth Gish.
- **TMDB:** movie/301804
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_I_Wake_(2016_film)); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/movie/301804).

### 9. Ouija: Origin of Evil (2016)

- **Type:** Feature film. 99 min.
- **Roles:** Director, editor, co-writer with Jeff Howard.
- **Producers:** Jason Blum, Michael Bay, Andrew Form, Bradley Fuller, Brian Goldner, Stephen Davis. Companies: Universal, Platinum Dunes, Blumhouse, Allspark (Hasbro).
- **Distributor:** Universal Pictures. Released October 21, 2016.
- **Budget / box office:** $9–12M; $81.7M worldwide.
- **Source material:** Hasbro's Ouija board game and characters from the 2014 film *Ouija* (Juliet Snowden, Stiles White). A prequel to the poorly reviewed 2014 film, and widely considered far better.
- **Premise:** In 1967 Los Angeles, widowed Alice Zander runs a fake séance business with daughters Lina and Doris, and adds a Ouija board as a prop. Doris starts genuinely contacting spirits and is possessed. The house hides a secret room where a doctor once held and experimented on kidnapped people, whose spirits now act through Doris. Father Tom and Alice are killed, and Lina ends up institutionalised. Lin Shaye's post-credits cameo as older Lina ties into the 2014 film.
- **Collaborators:** DP Michael Fimognari; music The Newton Brothers; cast Elizabeth Reaser, Annalise Basso, Lulu Wilson, Henry Thomas, Kate Siegel, Doug Jones.
- **TMDB:** movie/335796
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouija:_Origin_of_Evil); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/movie/335796).

### 10. Gerald's Game (2017)

- **Type:** Feature film. 103 min.
- **Roles:** Director, editor, co-writer with Jeff Howard.
- **Producer:** Trevor Macy (Intrepid Pictures).
- **Platform:** Netflix, September 29, 2017. Wikipedia gives the world premiere as BFI Southbank, September 19, 2017.
- **Source material:** Stephen King's 1992 novel. Long considered unfilmable; the adaptation is close to the book, keeping the eclipse backstory and the Moonlight Man/Joubert reveal, and dramatising Jessie's inner voices as visible figures (Gerald and Jessie herself). King called the rough cut "hypnotic, horrifying and terrific." The film sets the lake house near Fairhope, Alabama (per Wikipedia).
- **Premise:** Jessie and Gerald Burlingame go to an isolated lake house to revive their marriage. Gerald handcuffs her to the bed for a sex game, they argue, and he dies of a heart attack, leaving her chained with no key. Dehydrated, she argues with hallucinated versions of Gerald and herself, relives being abused by her father during the 1963 solar eclipse, and is visited at night by a tall "Moonlight Man." She escapes by degloving her hand. The epilogue reveals the Moonlight Man was real: the serial killer Raymond Andrew Joubert, whom she later confronts in court.
- **Collaborators:** DP Michael Fimognari; music The Newton Brothers; cast Carla Gugino, Bruce Greenwood, Henry Thomas, Kate Siegel, Carel Struycken, Chiara Aurelia.
- **TMDB:** movie/343674
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald%27s_Game_(film)); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/movie/343674).

### 11. The Haunting of Hill House (2018)

- **Type:** Limited series, 10 episodes, 42–71 min. First season of Netflix's *The Haunting* anthology.
- **Platform:** Netflix, October 12, 2018.
- **Roles:** Creator; directed all 10 episodes; wrote or co-wrote 4 (eps 1, 2, 6 with Jeff Howard, and 10); credited editor on one episode; executive producer.
- **Production:** FlanaganFilm, Amblin Television, Paramount Television Studios. EPs: Flanagan, Trevor Macy, Meredith Averill, Darryl Frank, Justin Falvey.
- **Source material:** Shirley Jackson's 1959 novel *The Haunting of Hill House*, adapted very loosely. Character names (Hugh Crain, Theo, Nell, Luke) and some lines (the opening paragraph) come from the book; the family drama structure is Flanagan's invention.
- **Premise:** In summer 1992 the Crain family moves into Hill House to renovate and flip it. Something in the house claims mother Olivia, and Hugh flees with the five children in the night. Decades later the adult siblings (Steven, Shirley, Theo, Luke and Nell) are each damaged in different ways. When Nell dies at the house, they are drawn back. Key reveals: the Bent-Neck Lady who haunted Nell all her life was Nell herself at the moment of death; the Red Room has worn a different face for each child; Hugh sacrifices himself to stay in the house with Olivia so the others can go free. Episode 6, "Two Storms," is built from a handful of long takes.
- **Collaborators:** DP Michael Fimognari; music The Newton Brothers; editors Jim Flynn, Brian Jeremiah Smith, Flanagan, Ravi Subramanian; cast Michiel Huisman, Carla Gugino, Henry Thomas, Elizabeth Reaser, Oliver Jackson-Cohen, Kate Siegel, Victoria Pedretti, Timothy Hutton.
- **TMDB:** tv/72844 (Hill House and Bly Manor are separate TMDB TV entries)
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Haunting_of_Hill_House_(TV_series)); [Wikipedia, Flanagan filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Flanagan_(filmmaker)); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/tv/72844).

### 12. Doctor Sleep (2019)

- **Type:** Feature film. 152 min theatrical; 180 min director's cut.
- **Roles:** Director, writer, editor.
- **Producers:** Trevor Macy, Jon Berg; Akiva Goldsman (who wrote an earlier draft) is an EP. Companies: Warner Bros. Pictures, Intrepid Pictures, Vertigo Entertainment.
- **Distributor:** Warner Bros. Premiere October 21, 2019 (Regency Village Theater, LA); international release from October 31, 2019; **US November 8, 2019**.
- **Budget / box office:** $45–55M; $72.4M worldwide.
- **Source material:** Stephen King's 2013 novel *Doctor Sleep*. Flanagan's approach reconciles King's novel with Kubrick's 1980 *The Shining*: it recreates Kubrick's sets and scenes with recast actors, brings the story back to the Overlook (which is not in the novel), and gives Dan the ending King wrote for Jack in the *Shining* novel (the boiler explosion).
- **Premise:** Adult Danny Torrance is a drifting alcoholic who gets sober in a New Hampshire town and works at a hospice, using his shine to ease the dying (hence "Doctor Sleep"). A girl named Abra Stone, with a far stronger shine, contacts him telepathically. The True Knot, a nomadic cult led by Rose the Hat, feed on the "steam" of shining children and are now hunting Abra. After Billy and Abra's father are killed, Dan lures Rose to the derelict Overlook, wakes its ghosts to consume her, and dies in the fire. Abra later sees Dan's ghost.
- **Collaborators:** DP Michael Fimognari; music The Newton Brothers; cast Ewan McGregor, Rebecca Ferguson, Kyliegh Curran, Cliff Curtis, Carl Lumbly, Zahn McClarnon, Emily Alyn Lind, Bruce Greenwood, Henry Thomas, Jacob Tremblay.
- **TMDB:** movie/501170
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Sleep_(2019_film)); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/movie/501170).

### 13. The Haunting of Bly Manor (2020)

- **Type:** Limited series, 9 episodes, 46–66 min. Second (and so far last) season of *The Haunting* anthology. Netflix said there is no story link to *Hill House*, though many actors return.
- **Platform:** Netflix, October 9, 2020.
- **Roles:** Creator; directed and wrote **episode 1 only** ("The Great Good Place"); editor on 4 episodes (per Wikipedia filmography table); executive producer.
- **Other directors:** Ciarán Foy, Liam Gavin, Yolanda Ramke & Ben Howling, Axelle Carolyn, E. L. Katz.
- **Production:** Intrepid Pictures, Amblin Television, Paramount Television Studios. EPs: Flanagan, Trevor Macy, Diane Ademu-John, Darryl Frank, Justin Falvey. Producers include Leah Fong.
- **Source material:** Henry James's 1898 novella *The Turn of the Screw*, plus other James ghost stories that give each episode its title: "The Great Good Place," "The Pupil," "The Two Faces" (parts 1 and 2), "The Way It Came," "The Altar of the Dead," "The Jolly Corner," "The Romance of Certain Old Clothes," and "The Beast in the Jungle." Loose; the core governess/children/ghosts setup is from *Turn of the Screw*, much of the rest is invented.
- **Premise:** In 1987 American au pair Dani Clayton takes a job at Bly Manor in England caring for orphaned Miles and Flora. The house is haunted by former valet Peter Quint, who used Miles's body, and former governess Rebecca Jessel. Underneath both is the Lady in the Lake: Viola Willoughby, a 17th-century mistress of the house whose rage has erased her face and turned Bly into a gravity well that traps the dead. Dani falls in love with the gardener Jamie. To save Flora, Dani invites Viola into herself, living with Jamie for years until the "beast" surfaces and she drowns herself in Bly's lake. The story is narrated in 2007 by an older Jamie at the wedding of a grown Flora, who doesn't remember.
- **Collaborators:** DPs James Kniest and Maxime Alexandre; music The Newton Brothers; editors Brian Jeremiah Smith and Flanagan; cast Victoria Pedretti, Oliver Jackson-Cohen, Amelia Eve, T'Nia Miller, Rahul Kohli, Tahirah Sharif, Henry Thomas, Carla Gugino.
- **TMDB:** tv/109958
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Haunting_of_Bly_Manor) (episode credits read from raw wikitext); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/tv/109958).

### 14. Midnight Mass (2021)

- **Type:** Limited series, 7 episodes ("Book I: Genesis" to "Book VII: Revelation"), 60–71 min.
- **Platform:** Netflix, September 24, 2021.
- **Roles:** Creator, showrunner; directed and edited all 7 episodes; wrote or co-wrote all 7 (co-writers James Flanagan, Elan Gale, Dani Parker, Jeff Howard). EP with Trevor Macy and Jeff Howard.
- **Production:** Intrepid Pictures. Filmed August–December 2020 in the Vancouver area (Garry Point Park in Richmond; town square in Langley).
- **Source material:** Original. Flanagan developed it as a novel from around 2010 before turning it into a screenplay and then a series. It is the book Maddie writes in *Hush*, and a prop copy appears in *Gerald's Game*.
- **Premise:** Riley Flynn returns to tiny Crockett Island after prison for killing a young woman while driving drunk. At the same time a young priest, Father Paul Hill, arrives to replace the ageing Monsignor Pruitt. Miracles follow: a paralysed girl walks, elderly parishioners grow young. Father Paul is Pruitt himself, rejuvenated by a winged creature (a vampire) he met in the Holy Land and believes is an angel. With zealot Bev Keane, he plans an Easter Vigil in which the congregation drinks poisoned wine, dies and rises as vampires. Riley, Erin Greene and Sheriff Hassan resist; the survivors burn the boats, church and houses so the turned islanders have nowhere to hide from the sunrise. Teenagers Warren and Leeza escape as the island burns.
- **Collaborators:** DP Michael Fimognari; music The Newton Brothers; cast Zach Gilford, Kate Siegel, Hamish Linklater, Samantha Sloyan, Rahul Kohli, Annabeth Gish, Henry Thomas, Igby Rigney, Kristin Lehman.
- **Awards note:** Bram Stoker Award for Superior Achievement in a Screenplay for "Book VI: Acts of the Apostles."
- **TMDB:** tv/97400
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Mass_(miniseries)); [TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/tv/97400).

### 15. The Midnight Club (2022)

- **Type:** Series, 10 episodes, 49–58 min. Planned as a two-season limited run; **cancelled by Netflix on December 1, 2022** after one season.
- **Platform:** Netflix, October 7, 2022.
